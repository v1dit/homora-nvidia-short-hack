TEXAS RENTAL PROPERTY REGULATIONS

STATE RENTAL LAWS:
- No statewide rent control or rent stabilization laws
- Landlords may increase rent with proper notice (30 days for month-to-month)
- Security deposits limited to reasonable amount (typically 1-2 months rent)
- No maximum rent increase restrictions

SHORT-TERM RENTAL REGULATIONS:
- State preemption prevents local STR bans in most areas
- Local municipalities may regulate through zoning and permitting
- Transient occupancy taxes vary by city (typically 6-17%)
- Austin: 90-day limit per year for unhosted STRs
- Dallas: No specific STR restrictions at city level
- Houston: Permits required for STR operations

EVICTION PROCESS:
- 3-day notice to pay or quit for non-payment
- 30-day notice for month-to-month tenancies
- No just-cause eviction requirements
- Landlord may enter property with 24-hour notice

PROPERTY TAXES:
- No state income tax - relies heavily on property taxes
- Average property tax rate: 1.8% of assessed value
- Homestead exemption reduces taxable value by $40,000
- Senior citizens (65+) receive additional $10,000 exemption

ZONING AND DEVELOPMENT:
- Generally landlord-friendly regulations
- Few restrictions on property modifications
- Accessory dwelling units (ADUs) allowed in most residential zones
- Mixed-use development encouraged in urban areas

INSURANCE REQUIREMENTS:
- Landlord liability insurance recommended
- Flood insurance required in designated flood zones
- Windstorm insurance required in coastal counties
- No mandatory tenant insurance requirements

HOA REGULATIONS:
- HOAs have broad authority to set rental restrictions
- May limit number of rental units in development
- Can impose minimum lease terms (typically 6-12 months)
- Architectural review committees control property modifications
